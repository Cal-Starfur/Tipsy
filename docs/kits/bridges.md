# Kit: Bridges and water in the town (contract v1, new)

**What this covers:** anything that carries a way over water -- a street, the boardwalk, a promenade or trail -- and the water it
crosses (THE PALMA RIVER's channel, `riverRuns` / `riverPieces` / `riverParapets` / `riverTiles` in `game/index.html`).

**Evidence:** Sir, 2026-11-25, on the river's first bridges ("lets learn first how to elevate road ways over water", then "lets dial
these bridges in and fix them and use the misses to make the rules"). Each rule below is one of those misses.

| ID | Rule | The miss it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| B1 | **Water in the town is sunk, never flat in the street.** It runs `RIVER.depth` below street height in a channel; the wall the eye sees (west on a north-south reach, north on an east-west one) goes down to it, with its wet line. A way over it stays at its own height, so it stands above the water: driving and the collision never change. | The river painted flat with the road laid on it read as a puddle, not a bridge. |
| B2 | **Every way over water is a bridge**, with all of it: a deck, a parapet along each edge over the water, its edge face (`kinds[k].F` thick) and its shadow on the water where the eye looks down the reach past it. Streets, the boardwalk, the promenade, a trail: no walk lies flat over sunk water. | The beach promenade ran over the mouth as a strip of gravel floating on the water; the boardwalk's south edge had no parapet or face. |
| B3 | **A street's deck is the street:** its road and both sidewalks, the sidewalks in the city's own slabs (`paveSlabOn`, on the city's T2 grid) out to the parapets, round a corner's outside too. Never a flat grey square. | The decks and the corner outside an L were flat paint; the sidewalk stopped short of the bridge. |
| B4 | **A street that ends at the water ends ROUND and kerbed**: its stub past the node cut back to a half round of the road's half width, the corners paved as sidewalk, the city's kerb (`kerbPath`) one stone round it from one side's kerb to the other's, the slabs on past it to the parapet; the surface and the kerb's wall follow the round (`riverEndSurface`). | A dead-end street's asphalt stopped square at the water with no kerb; then a square kerb round a square end ("the termination needs fixing"). |
| B5 | **Any street that reaches the water stands over it** -- a crossing, a corner the river turns at, a street's end -- so each gets its deck and its parapets (`perp`: any conn at the node). | The corner where a street dead-ended at a bend had no parapet on its open side. |
| B6 | **Quays are sidewalk**: the city's slabs, a coping along the water. | The quays were flat paint beside slabbed sidewalks. |
| B7 | **Layer order across the whole river:** the water, then the shadows and the walls, then every deck's edge face (it hangs below the street, so the bank in front hides its end), then every bank at street height (quays, coping, the sand, a walk running on), then every deck. What stands at street height is laid after the sunk water it hides; a deck after every bank, so no ground-level piece paints over a deck's edge. The sunk water shows through to street height `RIVER.depth` east and south of its edge, so every bank there is laid again over it -- a walk running on past a deck (the boardwalk east of the mouth) included. | The sand of the mouth's east bank painted over the boardwalk deck's corner; then the water showed through as a blue sliver on the boardwalk just east of its deck; then the decks' edge faces, laid last, poked out over the banks at their east ends ("draw order issue with the bottom pieces"). |
| B8 | **Nothing else stands in the channel or on a deck's edge**: the planting, the verge's sand and lamps, the hills' trees keep off the water (`riverHillClaims`, the verge's `rvCut`). | Palms and a lamp stood in the river at the mouth; trees and scrub grew in the falls. |
| B10 | **A deck edge is judged from the reach that has water past it.** Where two reaches meet at a node both see its deck's edges; an edge is used up only by the reach that walls it, never by one that skips it (nothing past it). | The bottom street's south parapet over the mouth went missing: the reach above had claimed that edge and skipped it. |
| B11 | **Shadows fall as the city's light does** (from +y and a touch of -x): a deck over a north-south reach shades the water NORTH of it, one over an east-west reach a sliver east; the channel's south wall shades the water along an east-west reach, its west wall a sliver on a north-south one. A core and a softer fringe, on open water only. No dark stripe down the middle of the water. | The deck shadows fell south, into the light, as hard dark bands, and a darker channel stripe read as more shadow ("shadows need some work"). |
| B12 | **A bridge is weighted to what it carries.** A street's parapet is a stone wall (`pt` 34, `ph` 78), the boardwalk's heavy timber, a footbridge's a light timber rail (`pt` 12, `ph` 44) at the walk's own edges, its deck face as thin (`RIVER.kinds`). | The promenade's footbridge carried a street's heavy parapets well out past its narrow walk ("weighted to the path size"). |
| B9 | **A parapet is pieces of `RIVER.piece` along**, each in the world queue at its own middle and cached (`bcDraw`, stable key) -- W37's long-piece rule. | (from the start: a 1000-long wall keyed at its middle sorts wrong round him at its ends) |
